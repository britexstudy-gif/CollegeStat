# CollegeStat UI audit and improvement plan — 13 October 2026

My assessment is **6/10 for the current product experience**: about **7/10 visually**, but **5–6/10 for quickly completing a student task**. These are subjective engineering/design judgments, not a user-study result or accessibility certification. The site has a coherent identity and useful sourced facts. Its main weakness is the effort required to find the right fact, distinguish its scope and turn it into a next step.

The university pages are **partly useful, not yet a strong application workspace**. Apply contains real historical admissions and scoped policies. Study and many international profiles remain thin. Costs leads with historical aid before the practical budget. Evidence exposes long technical lists. Better visual components alone will not fill these data gaps.

## What was examined

Fresh local read-only captures cover **61 distinct public URLs / 77 page-and-layout observations**, including all 16 original article readers, the six university primary sections and campus/gallery/research subviews, MIT, a sparse Uzbek profile and WIUT Apply/Study/Costs, the four world provider views, regional QS and HERD/NIRF, public comparison with actual MIT/Stanford records, scholarships, auth/recovery, support, sources/methodology/about/privacy/terms, aliases and 404/hidden admin behavior. Key pages were checked in light/dark at 390px and desktop at 1440px. Full-page captures were scrolled first so lazy images had an opportunity to load.

Private workspace and staff inspection used synthetic intercepted fixtures, not real sign-in credentials or private account contents. See [workspace audit](WORKSPACE-UI-AUDIT-2026-10-13.md) for its exact areas, scores and limits. Screenshots establish presentation, not real OAuth, AI availability, data publication or live message delivery. No hosted facts or schema were changed in this audit.

Public capture receipt: `outputs/ui-audit-oct13/public-receipt.json`; screenshots: `outputs/ui-audit-oct13/public/`. Initial preview/browser startup failures were retried. Correct provider URLs use `provider=the`, etc.; mistaken capture URLs are not counted as a product defect. The final receipt has zero failed captures, zero page errors and no document-level horizontal overflow. That does **not** mean the UI has no usability problems.

## Rating method

For each area I assessed practical usefulness, hierarchy/scan effort, navigation/action clarity, source/scope communication, and mobile/motion behavior. Scores below prioritize student usefulness; polished spacing cannot compensate for an absent programme, incorrect label or unavailable service. Private fixture scores are qualified in the companion report. Motion was captured statically with reduced-motion settings; existing motion implementation and earlier functional receipts were also reviewed.

## University pages, reviewed through their ends

**Overall useful-task score: 5/10.** Rich and sparse profiles share the same general structure, which is good. The content ordering, duplicate guidance and incomplete programme/applicant evidence make that structure much less effective than it looks.

| View | Score /10 | Observed issue / practical next design |
|---|---:|---|
| Overview | 6 | A next-step summary exists. Add a compact “Verified here / still unknown” snapshot and course/intake context; keep unknowns and official fallback links truthful. |
| Apply | 5.5 | MIT has useful rates/counts and language/test policies, but the page spans roughly 5,088px. Required/recommended/alternative/waived states need stronger distinction. Combine repeated guidance into one route/round/document checklist with evidence underneath. |
| Study / programmes | 4.5 | MIT has one verified current programme and 28 THE broad subject areas. These are correctly distinguished, but the breadth does not make a complete course catalogue. Explain “reviewed subset,” prioritize current degree details and add useful reviewed offers on existing institution IDs. |
| Costs & funding | 5 | MIT's roughly 4,163px page leads with historical financial aid. Show tuition/mandatory fees/living costs and exact year/population first, then aid/outcomes/history. Avoid a total/donut where periods or categories are incompatible. |
| Rankings | 6.5 | Exact installed publisher records are useful context. Keep provider/edition/scope separate; do not treat a position as a programme ranking or individual admission chance. |
| Research & people | 5.5 | Useful for students assessing a field, but broad aggregate research is not a course decision by itself. Connect sourced fields/departments to actual programmes where evidence exists; reveal full trends only on demand. |
| Campus/gallery | 6 | Permitted images and credits are preferable to invented assets. Show relevant campus/accommodation context; do not use image-search results without file-level rights. |
| Evidence | 4.5 after label fix | A roughly 7,328px technical list is useful for an auditor, not a typical student. Group observations by section/source with human-readable metric names, search/filter and expandable history. Keep every original observation available. |
| Sparse Uzbek profile | 3.5 | The page is honest but mostly missing fields and homepage fallback links. Offer a compact verified identity, explicit missing coverage and exact official links when reviewed; do not show a long pseudo-complete profile. |
| WIUT Apply/Study/Costs | 5.5 | The non-US sample demonstrates that useful course/cost/application evidence is narrower than identity coverage. Refresh and review exact pathways, applicant groups and cycles rather than assuming a nationwide certificate or fee. |

One strong profile should answer **What can I study? Which route applies to me? What must I provide? What will it cost? What is the next step?** It should answer those before presenting full research metrics, social links or history. “Unknown” is an acceptable answer; a guessed certificate, rate or deadline is not.

## Research informing the plan

Use a few essential choices first, with specialized/history/source detail revealed deliberately, consistent with [Nielsen Norman Group's progressive disclosure guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/). Motion must respect user preferences and not interfere with information, following [W3C animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions). [OnePrep's public college workflow](https://www.oneprep.com/college) offers a task-oriented reference for profile → application planning; CollegeStat should use its own identity and evidence model, not copy the brand, content, outcome claims or opaque admission-chance scoring. These recommendations are an application of those principles to the observed CollegeStat screens, not a claim that the private OnePrep app was accessed.

## Verification and limits

After the two targeted fixes, repository typecheck, lint, **572 Node tests** and the production build passed. The unchanged Python/data suite's latest result remains **217 passing tests on 12 October**; it was not rerun or relabelled as today's result. Public browser captures and two focused Evidence checks had zero page errors; private before/after checks used synthetic intercepted data. There were no hosted writes, new imported facts, schema changes, API-key activation, app deployment or repository push in this audit.

Existing optional large bundle/framework warnings, provider OAuth/email configuration, future hosted article authoring and much broader verified admissions/programme coverage remain work. Screenshot capture is not a performance benchmark, a user study, legal sign-off or exhaustive security test. Each of the 14,003 individual institution records was not manually browsed; the audit covers shared page families and representative rich/non-US/sparse records.

The initial large private development capture recorded **2,418 repeated “send was called before connect” errors**. Subsequent focused private checks recorded zero, but the original cause was not established. This remains a preview/runtime investigation item; it is not presented as a fixed production defect or a completely error-free private audit.
