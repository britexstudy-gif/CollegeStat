## In short

- Check the year, scope and measures behind a ranking.
- A whole-university rank may not show the strength of your exact course.
- Compare cost, entry rules, teaching and support as well.

## Read the method, not only the number

Ranking groups choose their own measures and weights. QS lists measures such as academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, international outlook and employment outcomes. Times Higher Education uses its own set of teaching, research, international outlook and industry measures. Rankings can therefore place the same university differently. Read the method for the exact edition you are viewing. [QS methodology](https://www.topuniversities.com/qs-world-university-rankings/methodology) [THE methodology](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/world-university-rankings-2026-methodology)

Check whether the table is global, regional, subject-based or limited to certain universities. Look at the data year and any notes about missing information. A ranking does not tell you whether you meet entry requirements, can afford the course, or will like the teaching style.

Use official course pages to compare what you will study, language, fees, location, support and graduate outcomes where available. In the United States, College Scorecard provides institution-level cost and outcome information; read its definitions and limits. [College Scorecard](https://collegescorecard.ed.gov/)

## What to do next

- [ ] Open the ranking’s methodology and check its year.
- [ ] Find subject or course information for your intended major.
- [ ] Compare cost, entry rules, language and student support.
- [ ] Keep rankings as one part of your decision.
