## In short

- Check the official pages linked below.
- Rules can depend on the university, country, course, and application year.
- Keep the dates and documents you need in one place.

## Make one comparable annual budget

Tuition is only one line. Build a 12-month budget with tuition and required fees, housing, food, local transport, books, health insurance, visa costs, flights, deposits and a reserve for unexpected costs. Separate once-only setup expenses from regular monthly costs so a low rent quote does not hide a large first-month payment.

In the United States, a school’s cost of attendance (COA) is a budget estimate that can include tuition/fees, housing/food, books, travel and personal costs. It is used in financial-aid calculations. It is not necessarily the amount billed by the university. Compare the COA parts with the school’s amount the university will bill you. [Federal Student Aid: COA](https://studentaid.gov/complete-aid-process/how-calculated) [College Board: College costs](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/pay-for-college/college-costs)

## Compare UK and Germany figures carefully

UK Student visa students must show living-cost funds at the level and for the duration specified by latest GOV.UK rules. That proof minimum amount is a visa requirement, not a complete prediction of personal spending. Check if the course is in London or outside London and read the latest amount and holding period on the official page. [GOV.UK: Student visa money](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)

Germany’s DAAD gives cost and funding guidance, while the separate 2026 study-visa blocked-account figure is at least €11,904. That visa amount is a proof-of-funds minimum amount and should not be treated as a guarantee that your chosen city’s actual costs will match it. [DAAD: Finance](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/living-in-germany/finances/) [Make it in Germany: Study visa](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying)

## Use this worksheet

| Line | Source to check |
|---|---|
| Tuition and required fees | University fee schedule for your course and year |
| Housing and meals | University housing page plus a realistic local rental estimate |
| Insurance and visa | Government and insurer pages for your route |
| Travel and setup | Your route, baggage, deposit and initial supplies |
| Aid or scholarship | Written award letter and its conditions |

Use a careful exchange rate, note the date, and check what happens if aid is reduced or housing costs more. Compare net cost only after checked scholarships. Treat loans and work income separately.
