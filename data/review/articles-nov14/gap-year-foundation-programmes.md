## In short

- A gap year is time away from study; a foundation course is a study programme.
- Set a clear purpose and budget for either choice.
- Get written proof that a foundation course leads to your intended degree.

## Plan a gap year

UCAS describes a gap year as time taken before or during a course of study and suggests thinking about how you will use it. You may work, volunteer, travel, or prepare for study, but check your university’s policy and your offer conditions. A gap year does not guarantee admission or a visa. [UCAS gap year guidance](https://www.ucas.com/undergraduate/applying-university/gap-year)

Write a simple plan with goals, dates, costs and a way to show what you did. If you already have an offer, ask the university if it can defer your start and whether you must meet any conditions first.

## Check a foundation course carefully

A foundation programme can prepare a student for a degree or help meet a university’s entry rules. The term does not mean that every course guarantees university entry. Check which university recognizes it, what grades you need, whether progression is automatic or competitive, and what happens if you do not meet the conditions. Some countries and universities assess school certificates in different ways. Germany’s DAAD explains that applicants should check the recognition of their exact qualification with the institution. [DAAD requirements](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/)

Compare course fees, living costs, language, refund terms and visa conditions. Ask for progression rules in writing before paying.

## What to do next

- [ ] Decide what a gap year or foundation course should help you achieve.
- [ ] Ask your target university about deferral or qualification recognition.
- [ ] Check foundation progression rules and total cost in writing.
