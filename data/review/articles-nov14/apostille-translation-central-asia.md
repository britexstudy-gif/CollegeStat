## In short

- An apostille confirms the origin of a public document; it does not translate it.
- The destination university decides what format it accepts.
- Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan use different services and authorities.

## Check the university’s instructions first

Ask the university whether it wants the original, a certified copy, an apostille, a sworn or notarized translation, or a record sent directly by your school. Ask which languages it accepts and whether translation must happen before or after the apostille. Requirements depend on the destination and document type.

Uzbekistan’s government page explains apostille steps for education and research papers. It names the competent authority for education documents and describes applications through public service channels. The official apostille register lets users check an issued apostille. [Uzbekistan education documents](https://gov.uz/ru/advice/616/document/2516) [Apostille register](https://apostille.gov.uz/)

Kazakhstan’s government lists an apostille service for official documents issued by education bodies. Its instructions describe online and in-person steps. A separate government page explains apostilles for other official documents. Check which service covers your paper. [Kazakhstan education service](https://www.gov.kz/services/3068?lang=en) [Other official documents](https://www.gov.kz/situations/110/349?lang=en)

An apostille is not a promise that a foreign university will accept a qualification. It confirms a signature, authority, or seal under the relevant process. Translation and academic recognition are separate questions.

## What to do next

- [ ] Get written document instructions from each university.
- [ ] Ask the school which office issued and can verify each record.
- [ ] Confirm the correct apostille service for each document.
- [ ] Use the translator and certification format the university names.
