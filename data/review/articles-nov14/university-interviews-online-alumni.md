## In short

- Read the university’s own page to learn what an interview means.
- Some interviews are with alumni; others use staff or subject tutors.
- Prepare to discuss your interests and questions, not a memorized speech.

## Know the format

Universities use interviews in different ways. Yale says an interview may be offered when available and is not required for every applicant. Oxford uses interviews as part of its academic selection process for many courses. MIT describes interviews with Educational Counselors, many of whom are alumni. Rules vary by institution and course, so check the official page and your invitation. [Yale](https://admissions.yale.edu/interviews) [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/applying-to-oxford/interviews) [MIT](https://mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/interviews/)

For an online interview, test your internet, camera, microphone, name display and quiet space before the meeting. If you have a time-zone or access problem, contact the university using the details in the invitation. Do not send personal records to an interviewer unless the university asks you to use an official route.

Prepare by reviewing your application and course choice. Be ready to explain an idea you found interesting and to ask a useful question. In an academic interview, the interviewer may ask you to think through a problem. Explain your steps and ask for clarification if you need it.

## What to do next

- [ ] Read the official interview page and your invitation.
- [ ] Confirm date, time zone, platform and whether attendance is required.
- [ ] Test your device and connection.
- [ ] Review your application and prepare two questions.
