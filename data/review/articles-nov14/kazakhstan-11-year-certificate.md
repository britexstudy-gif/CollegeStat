# Kazakhstan school certificates: 11 years, NIS and Bolashak explained

## In short

- No global rule says a Kazakhstan certificate does or does not allow direct bachelor's entry. The destination and university decide.
- For Germany, uni-assist has a specific instruction for attestats issued from 2015 through 2018, and accepts translations made in Kazakhstan only with a Kazakh notary's attestation.
- NIS graduates should ask each university how it treats their diploma and transcript.
- Bolashak is a national scholarship with its own selection, not a standard route for school leavers.

## Ask about your certificate, not only your country

Recognition depends on the destination's rules and on the course. Germany's DAAD points students to its admission database and stresses that the university makes the final decision. [DAAD: requirements](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/)

A specific note matters for Germany. Uni-assist says holders of the Kazakhstan "Zhalpy orta bilim turaly attestat" ("Attestat o srednem obshchem obrazovanii") issued from 2015 through 2018 must give personal proof of 11 years of schooling, such as the student's personal file. This is a period-specific instruction, not a rule for every recent graduate. [uni-assist: Kazakhstan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/kz/)

The same page asks for documents in the original language together with a German or English translation. It accepts translations from Kazakhstan only when a Kazakh notary attests the translator's identity and signature, and says this applies to translation agencies too. If you have already completed a year or more at a university, it also asks for an academic record for each completed year. [uni-assist: Kazakhstan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/kz/)

> **Example.** Aigerim's older cousin finished school in Almaty in 2017 and is applying to Germany. She gets her student file from the school, asks a translator to prepare an English version and has a Kazakh notary attest the translator, because uni-assist asks for that proof for certificates from that period.

## NIS

If you attended a Nazarbayev Intellectual School, ask each admissions office to identify the diploma and transcript issued to your cohort and whether the university evaluates them through a separate curriculum or school-certificate route. The NIS site describes its schools and programmes, but acceptance abroad is the receiving university's decision. Do not assume the NIS name replaces the formal review. [NIS](https://www.nis.edu.kz/en/)

## Bolashak

Bolashak is Kazakhstan's international scholarship, run by the JSC Center for International Programs. Its official materials define its own eligibility and selection, separately from university admission. [Bolashak](https://bolashak.gov.kz/en/)

The official site lists the Bolashak scholarship and scientific internships, and it now also carries news about a bachelor's track linked to international olympiads and science competitions. If you are an international olympiad winner, read the current rules on the Bolashak site and ask the Center directly. For everyone else, do not treat Bolashak as a standard admission document or a universal route for bachelor's studies. [Bolashak](https://bolashak.gov.kz/en/)

## The UNT and why some universities ask for it

Many universities abroad want to see more than your attestat. Some ask for your **Unified National Testing (UNT/ЕНТ)** result. In Kazakhstan the UNT is used for admission to universities and for the state grant competition; for 2026 the main UNT ran from 10 May to 10 July, with registration through the National Testing Centre site app.testcenter.kz or the UTO.kz app. [Ministry of Science and Higher Education: main UNT 2026](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/sci/press/news/details/1197627?lang=ru) The 2027 dates will be announced by the Ministry and the National Testing Centre; check there rather than relying on last year's calendar.

A UNT result can help show that your certificate leads to higher education at home, which some recognition systems look for. If a university lists "proof of a national university entrance exam", ask whether the UNT certificate is what it means. Keep the official result, and translate it with your other records.

## Translations, notarisation and apostille

Kazakhstan has been a party to the Hague Apostille Convention since it entered into force for Kazakhstan on 30 January 2001. [HCCH: Apostille Convention status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) Countries that are also parties generally accept documents with an **apostille** instead of full consular legalisation. Not every university asks for it, though. Before you pay for translations or apostilles, ask each admissions office three things:

1. Which documents need a certified translation, and into which language?
2. Do they want the translation notarised, apostilled, or both?
3. Should copies be certified, or will scans do for the application stage?

See also [Apostille and translation in Central Asia](/articles/apostille-translation-central-asia).

## If your school issues grades on a 5-point scale

Kazakhstan schools usually grade on a 5-point scale. Foreign universities convert it in different ways. Do not convert it yourself on the application unless the form asks you to; type grades exactly as they appear and let the university or your counselor explain the scale. If you studied at a school with a different system, such as NIS or an IB school, include the school's own description of its grading.

## Organise your documents

Keep one folder with:

- The original attestat and its grade supplement (the page with subject grades).
- The UNT certificate, if you took the test.
- Your passport, with the name spelled exactly as in the passport.
- Each translation, with the notary's attestation, as a separate file.
- For certificates from 2015 to 2018, the student's personal file and its translation.
- A one-page English description of your school's grading scale.

Name the files clearly in English, for example *Attestat_2027_EN.pdf* and *Attestat_2027_KZ.pdf*. Order translations of all pages at once, so the names and dates match across documents.

## A timeline for a student in grade 11

- **September–November:** list your universities and write down each one's document rules. Ask your school for an English transcript of grades 9–10 and a letter with your expected graduation date.
- **December–January:** apply where deadlines fall in winter, using current grades. Register for any English test or SAT your list needs. See [English tests](/articles/english-tests-central-asia).
- **Spring:** register for the UNT through the National Testing Centre if you need the result at home or abroad.
- **June–July:** receive the attestat. Order translations and any apostille at once, in the format each university asked for.
- **Before enrolment:** send certified copies or bring originals as the university requires.

## Questions to ask your school

- When will the attestat and grade supplement be issued, and can you give me a letter confirming my expected graduation before then?
- Can you issue an English transcript of my grades, signed and stamped?
- Who at school can fill in a counselor form or school report for US applications?
- If I graduated between 2015 and 2018, can I get a copy of my personal file?

## Checklist

- Send each university the exact certificate title, issue year, grading scale and transcript.
- For Germany, use DAAD and uni-assist guidance and the country notes that match your certificate year.
- Ask whether an entrance exam, university study, a foundation course or subject prerequisites are needed.
- Request translations in the format each admissions office requires. Uni-assist rules do not automatically apply elsewhere.
- Keep a copy of your student file if your certificate falls in the 2015–2018 period.

## Questions students often ask

**Does uni-assist accept a translation made by an agency in Almaty without a notary?** Its Kazakhstan page says translations from Kazakhstan need a Kazakh notary's attestation of the translator, including translations by agencies. [uni-assist: Kazakhstan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/kz/)

**I finished school after 2018. Do I need my personal file?** The uni-assist note names certificates from 2015 to 2018. For other years, follow the university's document list.

**Is an apostille always needed?** No. Many universities only check scans at the application stage. Ask before you pay.
