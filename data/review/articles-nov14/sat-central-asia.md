# SAT from Central Asia: register, find a seat, send scores

## In short

- Many colleges are test optional. Check each one before you pay for a test.
- Registration is through your own College Board account. Not every centre offers every date.
- International registration is listed at $68 plus a $43 international fee, so about **$111 before any extra centre fees**.
- Send scores from your account using each university's code and deadline. Up to four reports are free if you order them within nine days of the test.

## Do you need it?

Check each university's testing policy: some require scores, some are test optional and some use scores for scholarships or placement. Decide that first, then book. Our [balanced list guide](/articles/balanced-university-list) shows where to record it.

If you are unsure, write down three things for each college: "required", "optional" or "not considered"; whether scores matter for merit scholarships; and whether international applicants have a different rule. Some colleges set different policies for international students, so read the international admissions page, not only the general one.

## What the digital SAT looks like

The SAT is now digital and taken in the **Bluebook** app at a test centre. It has two sections, Reading and Writing, and Math, each split into two modules. The second module adapts to how you did on the first. The whole test takes a little over two hours, with a short break, and is scored from 400 to 1600.

You can use a calculator on the whole Math section, and Bluebook has the Desmos graphing calculator built in. You need a laptop or tablet that meets College Board's device rules, with Bluebook installed and the exam set up before test day. Phones cannot be used for the test.

If you do not have a suitable device, College Board runs a device lending programme. You register for a date at least 30 days away, choose "I don't know if I have access to the necessary device", then request a device from My SAT. An adult who is not a family member, such as a teacher or counsellor, must verify your eligibility. A request does not guarantee a device, so ask your school or family first. [Device lending](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/device-lending)

## Register

Sign in to your College Board account, choose a date and search centres by country and date. The locator warns that centres and seats vary, so search Tashkent, Almaty, Bishkek and Dushanbe separately and confirm the date before paying. [Registration](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration) [Centre search](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/test-center-search) [Taking the SAT worldwide](https://international.collegeboard.org/students/sat/taking-sat-around-world)

When you create the account, type your name exactly as it appears on the photo ID you will bring. For many students in Central Asia that is the Latin spelling in the passport, which can differ from a school record or a Cyrillic document.

## What it costs

College Board lists the international price as $68 plus a $43 international fee. Through June 2027 it also lists these extra fees where they apply: [International fees](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration/international-testing/fees)

- a $24 test centre fee at select locations;
- $34 to change test centre;
- $34 to cancel by the change deadline, or $44 to cancel later (up to the Thursday before test day);
- $38 for late registration.

Score reports beyond the free four cost $15 each, and rush reports cost $31 plus the report fee. Fees change, so read the fee page when you register. Pay with a card that works for international payments in US dollars, and check with your bank beforehand if your card is issued in sums, tenge, som or somoni.

## Choose a date

College Board lists these international dates for 2026–27, each with a registration deadline and a later late-registration deadline: [Dates and deadlines](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines)

- **5 December 2026:** register by 20 November; late registration by 24 November.
- **6 March 2027:** register by 19 February; late by 23 February.
- **1 May 2027:** register by 16 April; late by 20 April.
- **5 June 2027:** register by 21 May; late by 25 May.

Leave time for a retake and for score delivery before your earliest college deadline. For 2027 entry, the December date is the last one before most January deadlines. Spring dates are mostly useful for students applying for 2028 entry, or for colleges that accept late scores.

> **Example.** Daniyar in Bishkek needs scores for early-January deadlines. He finds that his first-choice city has no seat on the date he wanted, so he searches the next date and a nearby centre the same evening rather than waiting.

## When scores arrive

College Board publishes a score release date for each test. For example, it lists 18 December 2026 for the 5 December test, and 19 March 2027 for the 6 March test, so expect about two weeks. [Score release dates](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/score-release-dates)

## Send scores

College Board lets you send scores to colleges from your account. Follow each university's code and deadline, and check whether self-reported scores are allowed at application stage. [Sending scores](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/scores/send-scores-to-colleges)

Your first four score reports are free if you order them within nine days of the test date. After that each report costs a fee. [International fees](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/registration/international-testing/fees)

**Score Choice** lets you choose which test dates to send. Two catches: some colleges require all your scores, and Score Choice does not apply to the free reports you pick at registration, so those colleges receive that test's score whatever it is. [Score Choice](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/scores/send-scores-to-colleges/score-choice) Many colleges "superscore" by combining your best section results across dates. Check each college's policy before you send everything.

## How to prepare in eight weeks

- **Week 1:** take a full practice test in Bluebook. Note your score and the question types you miss.
- **Weeks 2–6:** practise daily for 40–60 minutes. Use the free official practice in Bluebook and Khan Academy's official SAT practice. Focus on the two or three skills that cost you the most points.
- **Week 7:** take a second full practice test under real timing. Review every wrong answer and write down why you missed it.
- **Week 8:** light review, sleep, and check your device, ID and centre address.

For many students from Central Asia, Math is strong and Reading and Writing is the section with room to grow. Read short English non-fiction every day: science news, essays, history. The test rewards fast, careful reading more than rare vocabulary.

## Step by step

1. Check each college's testing policy and write it down.
2. Create your College Board account with your passport name.
3. Check you have a suitable device, or start the lending request early.
4. Search centres in your city and one backup city for two possible dates.
5. Register and pay before the regular deadline to avoid the late fee.
6. Install Bluebook and complete exam setup before test day.
7. Sit the test and choose your free score reports within nine days.
8. After scores are released, decide whether to retake or send more reports.

## Test day

College Board's list of what to bring includes: [What to bring](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/what-to-bring)

- your fully charged device with Bluebook installed and exam setup completed;
- your admission ticket from Bluebook (a printed ticket is preferred; it is available after exam setup, from five days before the test);
- an acceptable physical photo ID (not a photo on your phone and not a photocopy);
- pencils or pens for scratch work;
- your College Board username and password.

A charger or portable battery is listed as "nice to have", since an outlet is not guaranteed. ID rules differ by country, so read the ID page for your testing country before the day.

## Before test day

- Match your account name to the ID accepted in your testing country.
- Recheck the centre, date and registration status shortly before the test.
- Keep your confirmation emails.
- If a seat is full, look at nearby dates and centres.

## Common mistakes

- Paying for the SAT before checking whether any college on your list needs it.
- Registering after the regular deadline and paying the late fee.
- Arriving with a device that has no Bluebook exam setup, or a flat battery.
- Picking free score reports for colleges that want only your best score, before you know that score.
- Assuming a self-reported score is enough when the college wants an official report.

## Questions to ask each college

- Do you require, consider or ignore SAT scores for international applicants?
- Are scores used for merit scholarships?
- Do you accept self-reported scores at application, and when is the official report due?
- Do you superscore, and do you require all scores?

## Short answers

**Can I take the SAT at school?** Some schools run in-school testing, but most students in Central Asia take a weekend test at a centre. Ask your school.

**Is one retake normal?** Yes. Many students take the test twice.

**What if I miss December?** Check whether your colleges accept a later score, and apply test optional where that is allowed.
