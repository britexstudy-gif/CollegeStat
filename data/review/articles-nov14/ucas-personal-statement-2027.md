## In short

- Answer three questions about your subject, studies, and preparation outside class.
- You have 4,000 characters including spaces across all answers.
- Each answer must have at least 350 characters; admissions teams read them together.

## The three questions

UCAS asks: why do you want to study the course or subject? How have your qualifications and studies prepared you? What else have you done outside formal education, and why is it useful? The questions help you organize the same personal statement. [UCAS guidance](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/the-new-personal-statement-for-2026-entry)

The total limit is 4,000 characters, including spaces. Each answer has a 350-character minimum. You can divide the remaining space in a way that fits your experience. UCAS says universities review your answers as one statement, so do not repeat the same point in each answer.

Use specific examples. Explain what you read, built, studied, observed, or learned, and connect it to the course. A short honest example is stronger than a broad claim. Check the live UCAS form for the current rules before you submit.

## What to do next

- [ ] Make notes for all three questions before drafting.
- [ ] Choose examples linked to your course.
