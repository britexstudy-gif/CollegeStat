## In short

- Compare the full cost, not tuition alone.
- Treat scholarships as possible support until you have a written award.
- Check course entry rules and language for each country.

## Compare the whole plan

Make one budget for each country. Add tuition, fees, housing, food, travel, insurance, visa costs, and personal costs. Use official pages from the university and government. A visa money rule is a minimum proof amount. It may not equal the money you will really spend. [Canada: proof of funds](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)

Then compare scholarships. Check who can apply, what costs are covered, how to apply, and when the award is known. Do not subtract an award from your budget until you receive it in writing. Check whether you must first apply to a university or if the scholarship has its own application.

Compare course language and entry rules too. A lower tuition price may come with a language course, extra year, or exam. Universities set many course rules, so check the exact programme page.

## Make a useful table

For each option, write down the university, course, yearly tuition, estimated living costs, confirmed aid, language, entry requirements, and application dates. Add a link and the date you checked each figure. Keep a backup option that your family can afford if a scholarship does not come through.

## What to do next

- [ ] Pick three countries and one course in each.
- [ ] Find official tuition, cost, and scholarship pages.
- [ ] Make a budget without assuming you will win aid.
- [ ] Ask each university about your certificate and language level.
