## In short

- Check the official pages linked below.
- Rules can depend on the university, country, course, and application year.
- Keep the dates and documents you need in one place.

## Give your school enough time and context

Ask the school counselor or school official early if they can send a transcript, school report and recommendation directly through the application platform. Common App’s first-year process includes recommender sendings, while UCAS requires a reference before the application can be sent. Each platform and university sets its own sending rules. [Common App: First-year students](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/first-year-students) [UCAS: References](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-university/how-get-ucas-reference)

Give recommenders a one-page information sheet: intended subject, activities, projects, responsibilities, deadlines, correct English spelling of names and an unofficial transcript for context. Ask them to write from direct knowledge and to distinguish school scale and grading conventions. Do not write or translate a recommendation and present it as the teacher’s own work.

## Build a complete study record

Keep the original-language certificate, transcript with all subjects and grades, grading scale, expected graduation date and any predicted grades. Uni-assist advises students to send complete educational certificates including subject and grade overviews, in the original language and with German or English translation as required. It publishes country-exact rules, which may differ from another university’s requirements. [uni-assist: certificates and translations](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/plan-your-application/educational-certificates-translations/)

Ask the receiving university if translations must be certified, notarised, completed by an approved translator, or sent directly by your school. Do not assume one country’s notary format applies to every destination.

## Apostille and legalization

An apostille authenticates the origin of a public document for use between countries covered by the Apostille Convention. It does not certify the truth of the grades or replace translation or university review. Check the HCCH status table for if both the issuing and receiving states are Convention parties and if an objection or special rule applies. Some destinations instead require consulate legalization or a different check procedure. [HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) [UK legalisation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised)

### Request this from admissions

“Please check the exact translation, certification, apostille/legalisation, and school-delivery rules for my [certificate name], issued in [country/year].” Save the written reply with the application checklist.
