# UCAS 2027 entry: dates, steps and what to ask your school

## In short

- You can apply to up to five courses in one UCAS application. The 2027 entry fee is £34.50.
- For 2027 entry, the main deadline is 18:00 UK time on 13 January 2027. Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses closed on 15 October 2026.
- The personal statement has three questions and a 4,000-character total.
- You need a reference, written in English. You cannot write your own.

## Dates

Applications for 2027 entry opened on 12 May 2026, and completed undergraduate applications could be sent from 1 September 2026. The equal-consideration deadlines are 18:00 UK time on **15 October 2026** (Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses) and 18:00 UK time on **13 January 2027** (most other undergraduate courses). Some courses use different dates, so confirm on each course page. [UCAS: 2027 key dates](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications)

Later dates in the same cycle, from the same UCAS page:

- **25 February 2027:** Extra opens, for applicants who have used all five choices and hold no offers.
- **30 June 2027, 18:00 UK time:** the last time applications are sent to universities; after this, new applications go into Clearing.
- **1 July 2027:** last day to add an Extra choice.
- **2 July 2027:** Clearing opens.
- **23 September 2027:** final date for 2027 entry applications.

Applying after 13 January is possible for some courses, but universities do not have to give late applications equal consideration, and popular courses may be full.

## Cost

UCAS lists the 2027 entry application fee as £34.50. It covers up to five choices, and there is no extra charge to use Extra or Clearing. [UCAS: 2027 application fee](https://www.ucas.com/faqs/what-is-the-application-fee-for-the-2027-cycle) Pay by card online. If your card is issued in Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan or Tajikistan, check with your bank that it allows international payments in pounds before the last week.

## Steps

1. **Research courses.** Compare entry rules, international tuition, English requirements and how each university treats your school certificate.
2. **Create a UCAS Hub account** and enter personal details, education and results. Type grades exactly as they appear. If results are pending, use the pending status.
3. **Choose up to five courses.** Research before you send. [UCAS: applying](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university)
4. **Write the personal statement.** For 2027 entry it has three questions: why you want this subject, how your studies have prepared you, and what else has prepared you. The combined limit is 4,000 characters including spaces, with at least 350 characters per answer. Universities read the answers as one statement, so avoid repeating yourself. [UCAS statement guidance](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)
5. **Arrange a reference** before the deadline. UCAS cannot send the application without it, and you cannot be your own referee. [UCAS references](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-university/how-get-ucas-reference)
6. **Pay and send**, then watch UCAS Hub and each university portal for interview, portfolio or document requests.

## Offers and replies

After you apply, universities send their decisions through UCAS Hub. You may receive:

- **Conditional offers:** you get the place if you meet conditions, such as final grades or an English score.
- **Unconditional offers:** you have met everything already.
- **Unsuccessful** or **withdrawn** decisions.

When all your decisions are in, you choose a **firm** choice (your first preference) and, if you want, an **insurance** choice (usually one with lower conditions). You decline the rest. UCAS gives you a reply deadline, so do not ignore the email. If you hold no offers, UCAS Extra and Clearing let you look for remaining places later in the cycle.

## Predicted grades from a non-UK school

UK universities make most offers on **predicted grades**. If your school is in Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan or Tajikistan, it may not be used to predicting. Ask your referee to:

- State your current grades and the grading scale.
- Give an honest prediction of your final certificate or exam results.
- Mention your rank or position in class if the school records it.
- Explain any national exams or olympiads you have taken.

Many universities also ask international students from these countries for a foundation year, a year of university study at home, or high results in specific qualifications such as A levels, IB or AP. Check the country-specific entry page of each university before you choose your five courses. UCAS also says you can get a statement comparing your qualification with UK equivalents from UK ENIC, but the university's own rule is the one that counts. [UCAS: entry requirements for international students](https://www.ucas.com/international/international-students/applying-to-university-as-an-international-student/entry-requirements-for-uk-courses)

## Your reference

UCAS says the reference must be written online, in English (or Welsh for courses in Wales). A teacher can add predicted grades and, with your permission, mention circumstances that affected your studies. Do not ask family members or friends: UCAS warns your application may be cancelled. [UCAS references](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-university/how-get-ucas-reference)

If your school is not registered with UCAS, you can still apply on your own and nominate a referee by entering their details in the reference section; UCAS then emails them instructions. If they do not respond, you can ask a different person. Request the reference as soon as your education history is filled in. [UCAS references](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-university/how-get-ucas-reference)

For a teacher who writes mainly in Russian, Uzbek, Kazakh, Kyrgyz or Tajik, offer help early: share your course list, your grades and two or three specific things they could say about you. The words must still be theirs.

## Writing the three answers

Each of the three questions should do a different job:

1. **Why this subject?** What draws you to it, with one specific example, not "I have always loved...".
2. **How have your studies prepared you?** Lessons, projects, olympiads, independent reading, and what you learned from them.
3. **What else has prepared you?** Work, responsibilities, activities or experiences outside school that show the skills the course needs.

Because the same statement goes to all five choices, keep it about the subject, not about one university. UCAS asks you to write original text, avoid quotes and clichés, and not share your statement online. [UCAS statement guidance](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)

Write in a document first, count characters including spaces, then paste into UCAS Hub and check that nothing was cut or reformatted.

## If you study outside the UK

Ask your school whether it can act as your UCAS centre and send the reference, or whether you apply independently. Give your referee one short packet: your course list, transcript, predicted grades and the deadline. Then ask each university in writing how it treats your national school certificate and whether it needs certified translations or records sent directly by the school. UCAS runs the application route; universities set the entry rules.

> **Example.** Timur is in Almaty. His school has never used UCAS, so he applies independently and asks his physics teacher to act as referee. He sends the teacher his five course choices, his predicted grades and the date 13 January 2027 in early November, and writes to each university about his Kazakhstan certificate before he pays.

## A timeline from now to the January deadline

If you are reading this in November 2026 and aiming for 13 January 2027:

- **Second half of November:** fix your five courses, add your referee in UCAS Hub, and book an English test if you do not have a score.
- **Early December:** finish a full draft of the three answers and ask one teacher to read it.
- **Mid-December:** check that the reference is in progress. Many schools close for the New Year holiday, so do not leave it to January.
- **First week of January:** final check of every section, pay and send. Do not wait for the last day.
- **After sending:** watch your email and UCAS Hub for interviews, tests or document requests.

## Organise your documents

- Your school certificate or transcript, with an official translation if a university asks for one.
- Your passport, with your name spelled exactly as in UCAS Hub.
- English test results, or the booked date.
- Any olympiad certificates or extra qualifications.
- A short list of each university's international entry page and the date you checked it.

## Common mistakes

- Applying to five courses whose entry rules you have not checked for your country's certificate.
- Mentioning one university by name in the personal statement.
- Adding the referee in the last week.
- Typing grades in a different format from your official documents.
- Forgetting that a conditional offer may also depend on an English score.

## Questions to ask your school

- Can the school register as a UCAS centre, or should I apply independently?
- Who will write my reference in English, and by when?
- Can the school give predicted final grades?
- Can the school send transcripts directly to universities if asked?

## Short answers

**Can I change a choice after sending?** UCAS has rules and time limits for this. Check UCAS Hub before you rely on it.

**Do I need an English test before I apply?** Not always. Many offers make English a condition you meet later. Check each course.

**What if I get no offers?** Extra opens on 25 February 2027, and Clearing opens on 2 July 2027.
