## In short

- You usually need an acceptance letter from a designated learning institution.
- You must show money for tuition, living costs and travel.
- Follow the live IRCC checklist for your case and country.

## Prepare the main documents

For most post-secondary applications, IRCC asks for a letter of acceptance from a designated learning institution. In most cases, you also need a provincial or territorial attestation letter. Quebec has a separate acceptance certificate process. Rules can have exceptions, so use the current official checklist for your situation. [IRCC documents](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents.html)

You must show that you can pay tuition, living costs for yourself and accompanying family, and travel. IRCC lists examples such as bank records, a student loan, scholarship proof, and a sponsor’s support letter with evidence. For a course longer than one year, explain how you plan to pay for the full course. [IRCC proof of funds](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)

IRCC may ask for identity, medical, police or other records. A study permit is not itself a travel visa. If approved, IRCC says it will also issue a visitor visa or eTA when needed for your passport. You must follow the conditions on your permit and show that you will leave Canada when your authorized stay ends. [Eligibility](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/eligibility.html)

## What to do next

- [ ] Check that the school and course meet current IRCC rules.
- [ ] Get your acceptance and any required attestation letter.
- [ ] Prepare clear proof of funds and the source of the money.
- [ ] Use IRCC’s live checklist and local visa-office instructions.
