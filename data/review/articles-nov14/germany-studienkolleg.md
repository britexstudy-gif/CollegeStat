# Germany: Studienkolleg, uni-assist and proof of funds

## In short

- First find your route: direct entry or a preparation course (Studienkolleg). The university makes the final decision.
- If your university uses uni-assist, follow both uni-assist's document rules and the university's deadline.
- For the visa, a blocked account of at least €11,904 (2026) is one accepted proof of funds. It is not tuition and not a full budget.
- Do not use procedures published for other countries, such as APS India, for a Central Asian certificate.

## 1. Find your route

Germany separates direct entry from cases where a preparation course is needed. Use the DAAD admission database or uni-assist's admission check with your country, certificate title and study history, then confirm with the target university. [DAAD: requirements](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/) [uni-assist: check admission](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/check-university-admission/)

Studienkolleg is a preparation course for students whose certificate does not yet allow entry to a degree, often with subject tracks and a final exam. Language and admission-test rules depend on the Studienkolleg. Apply early and check that your target university accepts that Studienkolleg and its final exam (Feststellungsprüfung).

## 2. Apply with complete records

For bachelor's applications uni-assist asks for the complete school-leaving certificate and the subject and grade overview, plus proof of any national university entrance exam you have passed. Country pages can require certified translations. [uni-assist: educational certificates](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/assemble-your-documents/educational-certificates/) [Uzbekistan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/uz/) [Kazakhstan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/kz/)

APS procedures run through country-specific Academic Evaluation Centres. For example, APS India verifies school or exam results issued in India, so do not assume an India procedure applies to a Central Asian certificate. Check the latest German mission checklist for where your records were issued. [APS India: FAQs](https://aps-india.de/faqs/)

> **Example.** Timur from Almaty gets an admission check result that suggests a Studienkolleg year. He writes to the university to ask which Studienkolleg it accepts and whether his subject track matters, then applies to that Studienkolleg first rather than sending money to an agent.

## 3. Proof of funds for the visa

"Make it in Germany" lists a blocked account with at least €11,904 for 2026 as one acceptable way to prove funds, and a scholarship or declaration of commitment may also qualify. The Federal Foreign Office notes that blocked-account providers charge their own fees and that the account must cover daily living costs. [Study visa](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/visa-types/studying) [Foreign Office](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service/visabestimmungen-node/sperrkonto-seite)

## How Studienkolleg works

A Studienkolleg is usually a two-semester course that ends with the **Feststellungsprüfung**, the assessment exam that, if you pass, gives you entry to degrees in the field of your track. Tracks have letter names, for example:

- **T-Kurs:** engineering, mathematics, computer science and natural sciences.
- **M-Kurs:** medicine, biology and pharmacy.
- **W-Kurs:** economics, business and social sciences.
- **G-Kurs:** humanities, German studies and arts.
- **S-Kurs:** languages.

The track limits what you can study afterwards, so choose it from the degree you want, not from what seems easiest. Many public Studienkollegs teach in German and set an entrance test (*Aufnahmeprüfung*) in German and maths; they usually expect solid intermediate German before you arrive. Some universities of applied sciences run their own Studienkolleg. Places are limited, so apply to more than one where you can.

## Do you need APS?

APS (Akademische Prüfstelle) checks are required for applicants whose school or university records come from a few specific countries, such as China, India, Vietnam and Mongolia. Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan are not on that list at the time of writing. If an agent tells you to pay for an "APS certificate" for a Central Asian certificate, ask them to show you the official German mission page that requires it.

## Costs to plan for

Most public universities in Germany charge no tuition for bachelor's degrees, but there are exceptions: Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students tuition, and some individual universities, such as the Technical University of Munich, have introduced fees for international students. Every student pays a **semester contribution**, usually a few hundred euros, which often includes a regional public-transport ticket. On top of that you need health insurance, rent and living costs. The blocked-account amount is the minimum for the visa, not a comfortable budget in cities such as Munich or Frankfurt.

## A realistic timeline

- **12–18 months before:** check your route on DAAD and uni-assist; start German if you need Studienkolleg or a German-taught degree.
- **9–12 months before:** gather certificates, translations and language certificates.
- **6–9 months before:** apply through uni-assist or the university. Winter-semester deadlines are often 15 July and summer-semester deadlines 15 January, but check each university.
- **As soon as you have admission:** open the blocked account and book the visa appointment; waiting lists at German missions can be long.

## Before you pay anything

- Verify the admission route and language requirements for your exact degree.
- Confirm whether applications go through uni-assist or directly to the university.
- Use the mission's latest visa checklist and book the earliest appointment it allows.
