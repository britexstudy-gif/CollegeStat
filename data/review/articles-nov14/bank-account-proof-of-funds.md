## In short

- Ask the university and visa office what financial records they accept.
- Show the money, its owner, and its source in a clear way.
- Opening an account does not by itself prove that funds meet a visa rule.

## Build a clear financial file

Rules differ by country and visa route. Official guidance may ask for bank statements, proof of a scholarship or loan, and a letter from the person supporting you. It may also ask you to show the relationship to that person and how the money is available. For example, Canada IRCC gives examples of acceptable evidence and says applicants must show funds for tuition, living costs, and travel. [Canada IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)

Check the exact amount, account age, statement dates, currency, translation and holding period for your route. UK Student visa rules have specific money evidence conditions. Read the current GOV.UK page rather than using another student’s old checklist. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)

If you need a local bank account in Kazakhstan, the government information for foreigners says requirements depend on the bank and describes an IIN, passport, local phone number and proof of lawful stay among the items banks may request. Confirm the current list with the chosen bank. This account-opening guide is not a student visa rule. [Kazakhstan government](https://www.gov.kz/situations/497/1153?lang=ru)

Do not move money just to create a large balance without keeping evidence of its source. Ask the bank, university, and visa authority how to document family support or recent deposits.

## What to do next

- [ ] Download the current official financial checklist for your destination.
- [ ] Ask your bank for signed statements in the accepted language and format.
- [ ] Add sponsor letters and proof of income or relationship when required.
- [ ] Explain large deposits and keep records of where the funds came from.
