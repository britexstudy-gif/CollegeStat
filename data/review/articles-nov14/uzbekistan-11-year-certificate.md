# Uzbekistan's 11-year school certificate: what to check before you apply

## In short

- There is no single worldwide rule on whether an Uzbek school certificate gives direct entry to a bachelor's degree.
- The destination country, the university and the course decide. Get the university's answer in writing.
- Prepare the complete final certificate with subject-by-subject grades, plus certified translations where required.
- An apostille is not accepted everywhere: Germany and Austria objected to Uzbekistan's accession to the Apostille Convention. Ask each university what certification it wants.
- A foundation year is one possible bridge, not an automatic requirement. Do not pay a pathway provider before the university confirms it leads to your degree.

## Who decides

An 11-year certificate records completed secondary education. Whether it counts as a university entrance qualification depends on the destination. Germany's DAAD tells students to check whether their certificate counts as a higher-education entrance qualification and says the university makes the final decision. [DAAD: requirements](https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/) For the UK, UK ENIC offers a Statement of Comparability, which compares an international qualification with the UK system; universities still set their own entry rules. [UK ENIC](https://www.enic.org.uk/)

## Documents

Prepare the final certificate with all subject grades, in the original language, plus translations. The uni-assist page for Uzbekistan asks for original-language documents with German or English translations. It accepts translations from Uzbekistan only when an Uzbek notary attests the translator's identity and signature, and says this applies to translation agencies too. [uni-assist: Uzbekistan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/uz/)

If you have studied at a university, uni-assist asks for an academic certificate for each completed year and the university's official grading system, including the minimum pass mark. [uni-assist: Uzbekistan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/uz/) Rules can differ outside uni-assist.

> **Example.** Sardor from Samarkand finishes school in June. In March he asks each of his three universities the same question in writing: "Does my Uzbekistan school certificate, graduating 2027 with these subjects, meet your direct-entry rule?" Two say yes with conditions; one says a foundation year is needed. He now compares real answers instead of rumours.

## When a foundation year appears

A foundation or pathway year can bridge a gap if the university says your certificate does not meet its direct-entry standard, or if you need subject or language preparation. It is not a universal requirement for Uzbek students. For Germany, check the DAAD or uni-assist admission information for your certificate, then confirm with the university because the database is guidance, not a binding decision. See [Studienkolleg and uni-assist](/articles/germany-studienkolleg) and [Gap years and foundation programmes](/articles/gap-year-foundation-programmes).

## Apostille, notarised translation or legalisation?

Uzbekistan joined the Hague Apostille Convention, which entered into force for Uzbekistan on 15 April 2012. [HCCH: status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) Several states objected to that accession, and between Uzbekistan and an objecting state the Convention does not apply. According to the depositary's record, **Germany and Austria** objected and have not withdrawn their objections; Belgium withdrew its objection in 2025 and Greece in October 2026. [HCCH: objections to Uzbekistan's accession](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications/?csid=1113&disp=type)

In practice this means:

- For **Germany and Austria**, an apostille on an Uzbek document does not do the job it does elsewhere. Follow the exact instructions of uni-assist or the university, such as notary-attested translations and certified copies.
- For many other countries, an apostille may be accepted instead of full consular legalisation. Check the university's list.
- Some universities only want clear scans at the application stage and check originals when you arrive.

Ask each university, in writing, exactly which of these it needs, and only then pay for translations and stamps. Order everything at once so the documents match. See also [Apostille and translation in Central Asia](/articles/apostille-translation-central-asia).

## What universities may ask for besides the certificate

- **Your grades for grades 10 and 11** (and sometimes 9), not only the final certificate.
- **Proof of a university entrance exam**, for countries that look for a "qualification for higher education". In Uzbekistan, admission information is published by the Agency for Assessment of Knowledge and Qualifications. [Agency for Assessment of Knowledge and Qualifications](https://gov.uz/en/uzbmb)
- **English test results**, unless you studied in English and the university accepts that. See [English tests](/articles/english-tests-central-asia).
- **School profile or counselor letter**, mainly for US colleges.
- **Academic lyceum or college records**, if you finished secondary education there instead of at a school.

If you finished an academic lyceum or a professional college, say so clearly. Some recognition systems treat these routes differently from an 11-year school certificate.

## If you are applying to the US

US colleges that use the Common App expect a transcript and school report from your school, usually uploaded by your counselor. Ask each college whether it needs anything more, such as a translation or an evaluation. Students from Uzbekistan are not rare there: in Common App's 2024–25 data, Uzbekistan was among the top 15 countries outside the US for first-year applicants. [Common App: What's new 2025–26 (PDF)](https://www.commonapp.org/files/Whats-New-25-26.pdf) Admissions offices have seen Uzbek transcripts before, but you still need to explain your school's grading. See [Common App steps](/articles/common-app-2026-27) and [Recommendations and school documents](/articles/recommendations-school-documents).

## A timeline that avoids the summer rush

- **Autumn of your final year:** ask universities your certificate question; request a school letter with your expected graduation date and an English transcript of grades 9–10.
- **Winter:** apply using your current grades and the expected-graduation letter.
- **Spring:** check whether any of your universities also need a national entrance-exam result, and register in time if they do.
- **June–July:** receive the final certificate, then translate it and arrange whatever certification each university asked for immediately.
- **Before enrolment:** send certified copies or bring originals as the university asks.

## Organise your documents

Keep one folder, with a copy in cloud storage:

- The original certificate and the page with subject grades.
- Your school's letter on expected graduation and the English transcript.
- Translations, each with the notary's attestation, as separate files.
- Lyceum, college or university records, if you have them.
- A one-page English note explaining your school's grading scale.

Name files in English, for example *Certificate_2027_UZ.pdf* and *Certificate_2027_EN.pdf*. Spell your name exactly as in your passport on every translation.

## Questions to ask your school

- When will the final certificate be issued, and can you give me a signed letter with my expected graduation date before then?
- Can you prepare an English transcript of my grades, signed and stamped?
- Who can complete a school report or counselor form for US colleges?
- If I studied at an academic lyceum, which document shows my full secondary education?

## Questions to ask each university

- Does my certificate meet your direct-entry rule for this course, or do I need a foundation year?
- Which documents need a translation, and must it be notarised in Uzbekistan?
- Do you need an apostille, legalisation, certified copies, or only scans at this stage?
- Do you need a national entrance-exam result?

## Checklist

- Ask each university for its exact English document list.
- Keep the original certificate and transcript. If grade pages exist, translate them too, not only the front page.
- Ask whether your school sends records directly, whether a notarised translation is needed, and whether legalisation or an apostille is required for that country.
- For Germany and Austria, do not rely on an apostille; follow the university's own instructions.
- Treat "11 years" as a question to ask, not an automatic rejection or an automatic foundation-year trigger.

## Questions students often ask

**Can a translation agency in Tashkent do my uni-assist translation?** Yes, if an Uzbek notary attests the translator's identity and signature; uni-assist says the rule applies to agencies too. [uni-assist: Uzbekistan](https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/uz/)

**Will the university reject me because I have 11 years of school?** Not automatically. Some accept it for direct entry, some ask for more, and some offer a foundation route. Get the answer in writing.

**I am applying before I have the final certificate. Is that a problem?** Often not. Many universities accept current grades and a letter with your expected graduation date, then ask for the final certificate before enrolment. Check each university's rule.
