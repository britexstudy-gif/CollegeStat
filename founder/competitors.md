**Buyer:** families in Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan (the parent pays, the student uses it) whose child applies for a bachelor's degree abroad for the 2027 intake.

## 4. Complaints, ranked

Public reviews of local agencies are almost all 5-star (4PREP 5.0, Everstudy 5.0, Instrux 94% five-star), so most complaints come from regulators, press and the few negative reviews.

1. **Paid, then nothing delivered, and no refund** (4 cases from 2 sources).
   - Uzbekistan's competition committee warned on 19 Jun 2025 about firms promising work or study abroad: "faqat pul olishgan, ammo ularning xizmatlari bajarilmagan" ("they only took money, but their services were not delivered"). It cites payments of "16 mln so'mdan ortiq", "36 mln so'm" and "2000 AQSh dollari ekvivalenti" ([raqobat.gov.uz](https://raqobat.gov.uz/uz/xorijda-ishlash-va-oqish-uchun-oson-borishni-vada-qilayotgan-konsalting-firmalardan-ogoh-boling/)).
   - Instrux, 1 star, 11 Nov 2026: "I paid 12,000,000 so'm for a Work and Travel consulting contract. The company provided no service to me." ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/instrux.uz)). This was Work and Travel, not admissions.
2. **Hidden or unpublished prices, extras added later** (seen across 7 of 8 direct players; 1 explicit warning, so the review evidence is thin).
   - "Некоторые агентства предлагают базовые услуги по более низкой цене, но взимают дополнительную плату за" extras ([edmasters.org](https://edmasters.org/articles/kak-vyibrat-agentstvo-dlya-postupleniya-v-ssha)).
   - Crimson's own pricing page gives no number and depends on "the level of support" (search-indexed [pricing page](https://www.crimsoneducation.org/us/about-us/pricing); live page unreachable).
3. **Very high cost and lock-in contracts** (thin: 1 company, several press sources).
   - Customers wanting to withdraw are "often locked in with their obligation to continue paying the fees"; Consumer NZ questioned the termination and "no warranty" clauses ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Education)).
4. **Cancelled or generic consultations** (thin: 1 review).
   - Crimson, 1 star, 5 Nov 2026: "8 hours before our meeting they suddenly cancelled it" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/crimsoneducation.org)).
5. **Free help is shrinking or narrow** (thin: 1 source).
   - EducationUSA Kazakhstan: "the previous regional virtual advising model administered by American Councils has concluded" ([American Councils KZ](https://kazakhstan.americancouncils.org/edusa-en)). EducationUSA covers only the US.

Reviews are too few to rank complaints by count with confidence. Themes 3-5 are thin.

## 5. The gap

**A small, published season price in Uzbek and Russian, with checked data per school, a refund, and a weekly report the parent can read.** Nobody found sells this.

Why this is the gap:
- Local agencies hide their prices (complaint 2), and the regulator has had to warn families about paying firms that deliver nothing (complaint 1). A visible price plus a refund is the fix for both.
- Published prices for real help start at £490 (SI-UK, UK only) and go to 399,990 ₽ and $25,000+. Below that there's only free information (Telegram, Studyportals, EducationUSA, ChatGPT) or a one-hour session ($99.99).
- Borderless's Kai Max at $300 to 1 Apr 2027 is the only season pass found, but its site is in Russian with no Uzbek version (borderless.so/uz returns 404). Its own description includes no parent view, no verified-data promise and no rehearsal of each portal.

**How narrow it is:** the gap is real but small. Borderless already proves families will see a "$300 season pass" offer, and it is a few features away from closing the gap. CollegeStat's edge rests on three things nobody else lists: an Uzbek interface, rehearsing each real portal (Common App, UCAS, uni-assist…), and a verified-data refund. The refund only works once the shortlist data is actually verified (`board.md`).

## 6. The threat

**Borderless is the fastest copier.** It has an Uzbekistan-flagged CEO, Russian content, Kazakh and Kyrgyz student stories, claims "11,000+" students ([borderless.so/ru](https://borderless.so/ru)), and already sells the same season shape at $300. Adding an Uzbek language, a parent summary or a data guarantee to Kai is quick work for them. If the Ready-to-Submit pass is priced at about $99 (the hypothesis in `offer.md`), it would sit below Kai Max. CollegeStat would have to win on language, parent trust and the rehearsal, not on AI.

Next threats:
- **yoonee.ai** plans Kazakh, Russian and Uzbek for the same buyer but isn't live.
- **Crimson** is hiring in Uzbekistan, but at a very different price.
- **ChatGPT** at $0-20 a month is the default substitute for students who would do the work themselves.
