# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market now exists.** Last review's top killer was "no small market to own". Choosing Central Asian bachelor's applicants for the 2027 intake fixes that on paper. It is narrow enough to dominate if anyone can reach it.
- **The start of proprietary know-how.** 204 target universities with verified routes, deadlines, tuition and IELTS minimums, plus 69 verified scholarships, is the first sign of work a copycat cannot rebuild in a weekend. Coverage is still partial: tuition for 77 of 204, IELTS for 44.
- **Language as a wedge.** Uzbek and Russian interfaces fit this segment better than an English-only tool would. Whether the main alternatives lack them is unverified.
- **A plan for search distribution.** Sitemaps, structured data, llms.txt and plain-English guides point to a low-cost channel. None of it is live, so it reaches nobody yet.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **There is still no distribution.** Nothing is hosted, there is no domain, no users and no chosen channel. Search traffic cannot start until the site is live and indexed, and the 2027 intake will not wait. Each month without users is a month the timing window closes.
2. **Breadth instead of depth.** Six languages, 14,003 universities, test prep, essay desk, form simulator and AI assistant spread one founder across eight crowded fronts. In each one the share is near zero. The only front where a large share looks possible is "verified admissions facts for Central Asian applicants", and that is the one with the biggest data gaps (international tuition, foundation years, Central-Asia-specific scholarships).
3. **No secret, no durable moat.** Data that is public can be re-collected. Without a stated insight, network effects (accepted students from the same schools and cities helping the next year) or a trusted brand in the region, a local agency or a better-funded platform could copy the data layer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many students in the four countries apply abroad each year, and what share could you reach by the 2027 deadlines? (Unknown; /founder-consumer for who they are, /founder-marketing for reach)
- Who already serves this student: local agencies, Telegram channels, school counsellors, global platforms? What do they charge and what do students complain about? (Unknown; /founder-competitors)
- What do you know about these students that those players do not? State it in one sentence.
- How do the first 100 students arrive, through which channel, at what cost each? (Unknown; /founder-marketing)
- Does the student or the parent pay, and does $9/month survive a cost to acquire each one? (Unknown; /founder-cfo, /founder-consumer)
- Can every one of the 204 targets reach full coverage (tuition, IELTS, deadline, foundation year) before you add another feature?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
"What else makes the app better" is, through this lens, mostly not new features. It is depth and reach:
1. **Go live and get real students.** Publish, buy the domain and put the product in front of real applicants from the segment. Measure where they came from.
2. **Finish the data where you can dominate.** Close the gaps for all 204 targets and find the Central-Asia-specific scholarships. "Every number you need, verified, in your language" is the one claim worth owning.
3. **Freeze breadth.** No new feature until coverage is complete and users exist. Hide what does not serve the segment.
4. **Build a network effect.** Start collecting outcomes (who applied where, who got in) from this year's students, to give the next cohort something no copycat has.
5. **Write the secret down** and run /founder-competitors to test it.
