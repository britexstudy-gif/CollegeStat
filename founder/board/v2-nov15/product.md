# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The segment is now real.** Central Asia to bachelor's abroad, 2027 intake, is a specific student. The landing hero says so. That fixes last review's first condition.
- **Depth started where it matters.** A 204-university target list with verified routes, deadlines, tuition and IELTS minimums is the right kind of work. It goes deep on what this student needs instead of wide across all 14,003.
- **The craft signal still holds.** Every figure is sourced, unknowns are never shown as zero, and scholarships carry a Central Asia eligibility flag. That is the "one thing" candidate, and it is getting sharper.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Focus was declared but nothing was cut.** The landing page still sells seven features: research, simulator, writing desk, plan, form practice, test prep and scholarships. The dashboard has about 16 sections, including people, certificates, report and assistant. The hero leads with "14,003 universities" rather than the 204 this student actually targets. The answer to "what else do we need?" is mostly less. The segment changed, but the product did not narrow with it.
2. **The main path still has holes.** Of the 204 targets, deadlines cover 89 (44%), tuition 77 (38%) and IELTS minimums 44 (22%). The student's first question is "can I afford it, can I get in, by when?" On most target pages, at least one of those answers is missing. Zero Central Asia-specific scholarships means the headline promise of the scholarship section is unproven.
3. **Still nobody has used it, and it is not live.** There are no users, no domain, no hosting and no installed email templates. The first five minutes have never happened. The app folder holds about 50 stylesheets, many of them dated "refinement" layers. Polish is being piled on rather than finished, and nobody outside has looked at it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: what does a student in Tashkent get here that they cannot get anywhere else?
- Why Spanish, German and Chinese, but no Kazakh, Kyrgyz or Tajik? Who reads the Spanish version?
- Whose first five minutes are you designing for, the student or the parent? Who pays? (*founder-consumer*, then *founder-pricing*)
- What are students in this segment using today instead: agents, Telegram channels, school counsellors? (*founder-competitors*)
- Which of the 16 dashboard sections did a real student ask for?
- How will the first 100 students find you? (*founder-marketing*, *founder-launch*)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut to one job:** "Find which of these 204 universities you can afford and get into, and what to do by when." Keep search, university pages, scholarships, the shortlist and the plan. Hide the following behind one "Practice" link or remove them: the test-prep bank, form simulator, admissions simulator, people, certificates and report. Cut the landing to three blocks. Drop the languages the segment does not read.
2. **Finish the slice.** Bring deadlines, tuition and IELTS to at least 90% coverage across the 204, or shrink the list to the universities you can fully cover. Show a clear "not yet verified" state, never a blank.
3. **Ship it.** Get the domain, Cloudflare and the installed email templates working, with one honest waitlist flow for Pro. A product nobody can reach is not finished.
4. **Watch 10 real students in the segment** go through the first five minutes. Fix what breaks before you add any feature. Freeze new styling layers until then, and consolidate the CSS instead.
