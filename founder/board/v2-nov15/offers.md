# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is now picked.** This was the top killer last time. Central Asian students applying for bachelor's degrees abroad for 2027 is a real segment with a fixed deadline. That deadline gives real urgency for free.
- **More reason to believe the research works.** There is now verified, sourced data for the target universities: application routes for 122 of the 204, deadlines for 89, tuition for 77 and IELTS minimums for 44. There are also 69 verified scholarships and Uzbek/Russian interfaces. That raises how likely a buyer thinks the research is to be right.
- **Less effort for the buyer.** One place, in their own language, replaces dozens of foreign university websites.
- **It still costs little to deliver**, so a large stack remains affordable.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Still no proof anyone pays, or that it gets anyone in.** There are no users and no revenue. Pro is free, and "Upgrade" joins a waitlist. On the value equation, likelihood is still close to zero. Nothing shows the app raises a score, finishes an application or wins a scholarship.
2. **The price was not reworked.** It is still $9/month, set against free tools. It has not been compared with what a family pays a local counsellor or prep centre for the same result, and nobody knows that figure (/founder-competitors). A monthly plan in a business that runs in seasons invites people to cancel after the deadline. The payer is still undecided, and a parent buys a different offer from a student (/founder-consumer).
3. **Still a feature list, not an offer.** Since the last review, more features were added: a form simulator, guides, logos, discoverability work. None of them became a named result with a deadline and a guarantee. The founder's question, "what else do we need?", assumes the answer is more features. Through this lens, the missing piece is the offer, not the app.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who signs the payment, the parent or the student? What does that person pay a local counsellor today? (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors)
- What single result can you promise for the 2027 intake, and by what date? Example: "a shortlist of 15 schools you can afford, with deadlines and scholarships, in 7 days."
- What guarantee could you survive, and what would it cost you? (/founder-offer, /founder-cfo)
- Would a one-off season pass sell better than a monthly plan? At what price? (/founder-pricing)
- Can you get even 5 families to pay real money before Pro is built further?
- Of the data gaps, international tuition and foundation years are the main ones. Which do buyers care about most? Is there a single scholarship that fits this segment closely enough to name it in the offer? (/founder-consumer)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Name the offer.** Make one outcome tied to the 2027 deadline, the core offer. Example: a personalised shortlist that matches the family's budget, IELTS level and the scholarships open to them, plus an application calendar. Stack only the parts that serve that outcome. Add a guarantee the CFO approves. (/founder-offer, /founder-cfo)
2. **Price against the human alternative.** Choose the payer, and test a season price against counsellor fees, not against free tools. (/founder-pricing, after /founder-consumer)
3. **Get real proof before more features.** Go live on a domain. Get at least 5 paid buyers in Uzbekistan or Kazakhstan and one measurable result each, such as a finished shortlist or a submitted application. Freeze new features until then.
4. **Close the data gap the offer depends on.** If the promise is "schools you can afford", get tuition data for every school on the shortlist.
