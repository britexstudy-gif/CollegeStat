# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pain is real and comes in seasons: university admission is a high-stakes, one-shot outcome, and families do spend on it (test prep, counsellors, application fees).
- The dream outcome is easy to state: "get into the right university, with funding."
- The data honesty (sources, years, no fake zeros) could help buyers believe the research side works.
- One profile feeding every tool cuts effort. That is the right instinct for the effort part of the value equation.
- Cheap to deliver: software plus AI, so a big stack costs little at the margin.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No market has been picked.** "High-school students worldwide" is not a market. Buyers have very different pain, budgets and reach depending on country and income. Students often have no money, so the paying parent has to be found and convinced. Until one segment is chosen, nobody can say whether buyers are easy to find or can pay.
2. **Weak on likelihood and price-led.** Nothing shows that the product gets anyone admitted or raises scores. The practice tests say outright that they predict no score. With no proof, no guarantee and no results, $9/month only works by being cheaper. In a crowded field of free tools and paid prep, being cheaper makes it a commodity.
3. **A feature list, not an offer.** Eight sections are spread across every stage from discovery to "share your experience". There is no named result, no deadline, no guarantee and no real urgency, even though admission deadlines would give real urgency for free. Pro is free right now, so nobody has shown they will pay.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single buyer (country, school year, income, test) has the most pain and the money to pay? Who pays: the student or the parent?
- What specific result can you promise, by when? For example, "IELTS 7.0 in 8 weeks" or "a finished application list and essays before the deadline".
- What proof do you have that it works? What guarantee could you offer and survive?
- What would a human counsellor or prep course charge this buyer for the same result? Could you charge 5–10x $9 and still be the obvious choice?
- What do you have that free university search sites and official test-prep material do not?
- Is it a monthly subscription or an offer sold per application season? Admissions run in seasons, so monthly churn after deadlines is a risk. What do you expect retention to be? (Unknown.)
- How much does it cost to reach one paying parent in the chosen segment? (Unknown.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one narrow segment, for example one country, one test, one admission cycle, and name the buyer who pays.
2. Rebuild Pro as a named offer tied to an outcome and a deadline. Stack only the parts that serve that outcome, add a real guarantee, and price it against the human alternative rather than against free tools.
3. Get real proof: at least a handful of users who paid real money (not a waitlist) and measurable results such as score gains or applications submitted, before any further feature work.
