# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A possible know-how edge in data honesty.** Every figure carries its source, year and currency, and unknowns are never shown as zero. If this can be done reliably across 14,000+ universities and kept current, it is the closest thing here to proprietary know-how. Today it is a principle, not a proven advantage.
- **A single profile across all tools** could become a switching cost: once a student's tests, essays and plan live in one place, leaving costs them something.
- **The Community vision** (accepted students, reviews) is the only part that could create network effects. It is not built yet.
- **Costs are low.** A solo founder working with AI agents can stay alive long enough to search for a niche.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No small market to own.** "High-school students worldwide, applying anywhere" is the largest and most crowded version of this market. University search, test prep, essay help and AI advisors each already have established players and free alternatives within reach of the same student. The product starts with a share near zero in each of eight areas instead of a big share of one.
2. **No distribution.** There is no channel, no users, no research, and no hosting in production. The cost of reaching a student is an open question, and a $9/month product aimed at teenagers leaves little room to pay for them. Students also leave once they have applied, so each customer is lost within a year or two.
3. **No secret and no moat.** Combining public data with practice tests and an LLM advisor is an incremental improvement, not a breakthrough. A well-funded incumbent, or another solo builder with the same AI tools, could copy what exists in month six. The project's speed of building (one month) is exactly the problem: copying it would be just as fast.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which narrow segment (one country, one destination, one exam, one income band) could you dominate within 12 months, and how many students are in it? (Unknown)
- What do you know about this market that the established platforms do not?
- Who else is within reach of that exact student today, and why would they switch?
- How do the first 100 students arrive, and what does each one cost? (Unknown)
- Why now? What has changed that makes this possible or needed today?
- What is still yours in ten years: the data, the community, or the brand?
- How do you keep 14,000 university records accurate as a solo founder, and is that cost an advantage or a burden?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). To come back: pick one niche you can own, state the secret, and show a distribution channel with real users and a measured acquisition cost.
