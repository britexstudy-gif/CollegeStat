# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Taste in a place nobody looks.** Every figure keeps its source, year and currency, and unknown values are never shown as zero. That is real craft in a category full of made-up numbers, and it is the best candidate for the "one thing."
- **A clear organising principle.** "Enter your information once, use it everywhere" describes an experience, not a feature list. It is the right instinct.
- **Honest framing of practice content.** Labelling it as not official tests and not score predictions keeps a seam from turning into a broken promise.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** Eight sections, four exam types, essays, simulators, scholarships, community, AI advisor, built for "high-school students, including internationally." This is a suite with no centre. The Product lens asks what to cut, and right now the answer is "nothing has been cut." A product that does ten things adequately loses to ten products that each do one thing well.
2. **Depth does not match breadth, and users will hit the gap in minute one.** 14,000+ universities sit next to 7 scholarships and 20 dates. A student who searches for their target school or their scholarship and finds thin or stale data meets the seam on first use. How many of the 14,000 profiles are complete and current is an open question.
3. **No one has used it.** No user research, no hosting, no email, no payments. The first-five-minutes experience has only been imagined. One month of agent-driven building produced a wide surface. Nobody yet knows if the parts nobody sees (data freshness, error states, account recovery) hold up.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what does CollegeStat do better than anything else? If it takes a paragraph, it is not clear yet.
- Who is the single student you are building for: what country, what school year, applying where?
- Walk me through their first five minutes. What do they type, what do they see, and why do they come back tomorrow?
- Of the 14,000 university profiles, how many are complete, sourced and from this cycle? Who keeps them current?
- Which five of the eight sections would you remove today if you had to?
- Where does the experience pass between systems (AI advisor to profile, mock to study plan, waitlist to paid) and what breaks there?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut to one segment and one job. Write the one-sentence pitch, and hide every section that does not serve it.
2. Make the chosen slice complete. That means full, current, sourced data for the universities and scholarships that segment actually targets, with no empty states on the main path.
3. Put it in front of 10–20 real students from that segment. Record their first five minutes and fix what breaks before building anything new.
4. Close the visible seams on the main path: hosting, sign-in, email, and an honest upgrade flow.
