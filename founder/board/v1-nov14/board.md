# CollegeStat: board verdict

**Vote: 2 fund if, 1 pass. Average score 3.3 / 10.**

| Lens | Score | Vote |
|---|---|---|
| Offers | 3 | FUND IF |
| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |
| Product | 4 | FUND IF |

## Risks raised by more than one member

1. **No chosen segment (all three).** "High-school students, including internationally" is the biggest, most crowded version of the market. Until one country, exam and cycle is picked, nobody can judge reach, ability to pay or competition.
2. **Breadth with no centre (all three).** Eight sections across every stage from discovery to community. It reads as a feature list, not an offer (Offers), a small share of eight markets instead of a big share of one (Monopoly), and a suite with nothing cut (Product).
3. **No users, no payments, no distribution (all three).** No user research, no production hosting, no acquisition channel. Pro is free during early access, so nobody has shown they will pay.
4. **Data honesty is the best asset, but not yet proven at scale (Monopoly, Product).** Source, year and currency on every figure stands out in this category. But 14,000 universities sit beside 7 scholarships and 20 dates. How many profiles are complete and current is unknown, and keeping them current alone could become a burden.
5. **Seasonal churn (Offers, Monopoly).** Students leave after applying, which works against a $9/month subscription.

## Where the members disagree

- **Monopoly passed; the other two said "fund if".** The Monopoly lens sees no lasting advantage: anything built in one month with AI agents can be copied in one month. The Offers and Product lenses think a narrow, deep version is still worth backing.
- **Price.** The Offers lens thinks $9/month is too *low*: priced against free tools, it should be priced against a human counsellor. The Monopoly lens worries the price leaves no room to pay for acquiring students.

## Conditions checklist

- [ ] Pick one segment: country, exam, school year, admission cycle. Name who pays, the student or the parent. (all)
- [ ] Write the one-sentence pitch and hide every section that doesn't serve it. (Product, Offers)
- [ ] Make that slice complete: full, current, sourced data for the universities and scholarships the segment targets, with no empty states on the main path. (Product)
- [ ] Rebuild Pro as a named result with a deadline and a guarantee, priced against a human counsellor or prep course, possibly sold per season instead of monthly. (Offers) → `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Put it in front of 10–20 real students and record their first five minutes. (Product) → `/founder-consumer` first, then real users
- [ ] Get a handful of real paying users and measurable results before more feature work. (Offers)
- [ ] Find one acquisition channel and measure what each student costs. (Monopoly) → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-marketing`
- [ ] Ship the basics on the main path: hosting, sign-in, email, an honest upgrade flow. (Product)
- [ ] State the secret: what you know about this market that the established platforms don't. (Monopoly) → `/founder-competitors`

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from what was pitched. Not a worldwide eight-section university operating system, but **the most trustworthy application guide for one specific group**. For example: students from one country applying abroad for one intake, a group whose options, costs, scholarships and deadlines are scattered, out of date or made up elsewhere. CollegeStat would cover only the universities and funding they actually target, with every figure sourced and current. The writing desk and one test would be tied to their real deadlines and sold as a per-season package rather than a monthly subscription. Data honesty is the one thing all three lenses praised. In a narrow slice it can be both complete and defensible; across 14,000 universities it is neither yet. Everything else (community, AI advisor, the other exams) waits until that slice has paying users.
