## What changed, and why the score did not move
- **Credited:** the site is live and indexed (a condition from last time). The practice application in each university's real portal, ending in a decision letter, is named by all three lenses as the strongest and least copyable product moment so far. The paywall at the AI limit puts the upgrade at a moment of real need. The guides now exist in Russian and Uzbek.
- **Cancelling it out:** the board asked for a feature freeze, deployment and proof. The sprint added more features instead (an AI agent with tools, a calendar, a report, a longer onboarding, avatars, a tour), most not deployed. No users, sign-ups or paywall numbers were reported.

## Risks raised by more than one member
1. **No proof of demand (all three).** No reported visitors, sign-ups, returning users, paywall hits or payments. "Upgrade" still joins a waitlist. Nothing shows a family believes this gets their child admitted or funded.
2. **Breadth grew again, after a freeze was asked for (all three).** The landing page still sells many things to the same student. Each feature is well made, but together they don't name one thing.
3. **The core data under the best feature is still partial (Product, Monopoly, Offers).** Last known coverage for the 204 targets: deadlines 44%, tuition 38%, IELTS 22%. The 50-value import is not applied. Central Asia-specific scholarships: none found. The decision letter can sound confident on an empty base.
4. **Offer and price are not settled (Offers, Product).** The brief said $9 Pro; the live site shows Scholar $6 and Admit $15. There's no named result, no guarantee, no season price, and the payer (parent or student) is still undecided.
5. **No moat or channel (Monopoly, Offers).** Public data and a third-party AI can be matched. No channel beyond slow SEO, with fixed 2027 deadlines.

## Conditions (merged checklist)
- [ ] **Deploy what is built, then freeze features.** No new features until the items below are done. (all)
- [ ] **Measure:** report visits, sign-ups, onboarding completion, returning users, paywall hits and waitlist joins, by source. (all)
- [ ] **Watch 10 real students** from the segment use the live site, and fix what breaks for them. (Product)
- [ ] **Pick the one thing and lead with it**, e.g. "Practise your real application to the universities you can afford, and see what to fix." Cut or hide the rest. (Product, Offers)
- [ ] **One price story** everywhere (brief, landing, FAQ, paywall). Choose the payer and test a season price against what a human counsellor costs. (/founder-pricing, /founder-offer)
- [ ] **Take real money from 5 families**, by hand if needed, before wiring payments. (Offers)
- [ ] **Finish the data slice:** apply the import; reach 90%+ coverage of deadlines, tuition and IELTS for the 204 targets, or shrink the list. Show "not verified" instead of a verdict when data is missing. (Product, Offers, Monopoly)
- [ ] **One channel beyond SEO** where these students gather (e.g. Telegram), and the first 100 users through it. (/founder-marketing)
- [ ] **Start the network effect:** record this cohort's applications and results for the next cohort. (Monopoly)
- [ ] **Write the secret down** in one sentence: what you know about Central Asian applicants that agencies and global tools don't. (Monopoly)

## Where the members disagree
- **What to keep:** Product would lead with the practice application. Monopoly sees the native-language guides and SEO as the best wedge. Offers would build the offer around a deadline result (an affordable shortlist plus a practice application per school).
- **How hard to cut:** Product wants features removed from view; Offers mainly wants them stacked under one named result.

## The strongest version the board can see
A Russian- and Uzbek-language service for families in Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan whose child applies abroad for 2027. It does one job: build an affordable, verified shortlist (deadline, tuition and IELTS confirmed for every school), then rehearse each real application in the portal it uses, so nothing surprises them on submission day. It is sold once per application season to the parent, at a price set against local counsellors, not against free sites. The guides and SEO bring families in, and a Telegram channel keeps them. This year's results become next year's data. It's narrower than what has been built: most other features become supporting parts or wait.
